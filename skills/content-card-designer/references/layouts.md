# Layouts and sequencing

## Choosing a layout

| Content | Layout | Keep it to |
|---|---|---|
| A hook for the first card | `cover` | A title of two short lines; one line of subtitle; up to five chips |
| Several parallel items, each with a label | `list` | 3 to 5 rows |
| A sequence or two small groups | `steps` | 3 to 4 steps; a short group of pills or chips |
| Short statements that each need a sentence | `points` | 2 to 4 boxes |
| One point per card, numbered (a tips series) | `point` | A number, a title, and at most one supporting element the user supplied |
| Two options, before and after, do and don't | `compare` | 3 to 4 items per side |
| One number that matters | `stat` | The number, a caption, up to three notes. The number must come from the user. |
| A single sentence worth reading slowly | `quote` | Under about 30 characters of Chinese or 15 words |
| A closing action | `cta` | Up to three steps and one note |
| A strong picture with a short headline | `image` | Headline and one line; text on the clear side |
| A real product, screenshot or photo | `product` | The image, a short title, up to three bullets |

## A reliable carousel shape

1. **Cover** (`cover`, or `image` if there is a good picture): promise or question.
2. **Context or claim** (`points`, `quote`): why this matters, in one idea.
3. **Substance** (`list`, `steps`, `compare`, `stat`): the content itself. Alternate layouts.
4. **Proof or example** (`stat`, `quote`, `product`) when the user has real material for it.
5. **Close** (`cta`): what to do next. Skip it if the user has no action to offer.

Three to nine cards covers most needs. Fewer, sharper cards beat more crowded ones.

## Writing for cards

- **One claim per card.** If a card makes two points, split it.
- **Cut, don't shrink.** Fit by cutting words; the fitter will not go below the minimum font.
- **Front-load.** The first words of each title should carry the point.
- **Keep the user's facts.** Tighten wording for length, but never change a number, name or claim. Tell the user what you shortened.
- **No filler.** No "swipe for more", no invented statistics, no fake testimonials.

## Wide and square canvases

The same spec works across formats because each canvas is composed again, not cropped: lists become two columns on wide canvases, covers put the chips beside the title, product cards place the image beside the text. If a layout looks wrong on a format, say so and pick another rather than forcing it.
